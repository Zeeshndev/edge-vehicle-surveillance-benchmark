## Differentiation from Prior Work (Draft)
Recent literature has extensively benchmarked object detection energy consumption on dedicated embedded hardware, such as Raspberry Pi and Jetson Orin Nano clusters (Toosi et al., 2024). Concurrently, earlier mobile-focused studies have characterized the battery and latency tradeoffs of smartphone object detection, largely focusing on cloud-offloading paradigms to handle the computational weight of older YOLO architectures (Chen et al., 2017). Our empirical study bridges and updates both domains. We isolate consumer Android hardware as the deployment target, completely eliminate the offloading paradigm, and benchmark the absolute newest lightweight architectures (YOLOv10n and YOLOv11n). By relying entirely on INT8 post-training quantization, we provide a modern baseline for 100% on-device, privacy-preserving vehicle surveillance.
